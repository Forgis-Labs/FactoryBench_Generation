\begin{abstract}
    Industrial robots emit dense multivariate telemetry that determines their operating state, and acting on a fault requires both reading that state and knowing the procedure that resolves it. These are separate abilities, and they are usually measured separately: perception benchmarks stop at the reading, while control benchmarks score the resulting action without isolating which of the two failed. We introduce \textbf{FactoryBench}, a benchmark that measures both on the same episodes from real industrial robots, so that physical perception and physically grounded decision-making can be told apart. Q\&A pairs are organized along four levels, physical state estimation, intervention, counterfactual reasoning, and engineering decision-making, and span five answer formats: four are scored deterministically and free-form answers by an LLM-as-judge voting protocol. We release \textbf{FactoryWave} (a dense, multitask, multivariate sensor dataset from a UR3 cobot and a KUKA KR10 industrial arm, with counterfactual episodes recorded on hardware by re-executing a task under held-fixed conditions to approximate the do-operation) and build FactoryBench as over 70k Q\&A items grounded in roughly 15k normalized episodes from FactoryWave, AURSAD~\cite{leporowski2022aursad}, and voraus-AD~\cite{brockmann2023vorausad}. Evaluating six frontier models, none exceeds 50\% (chance-corrected) on the perception-side levels, and the ranking reshuffles entirely at the decision level: the leader on the first three levels falls to the bottom of the fourth. Linear probes show this is not one deficit but two, recovering fault presence from a frozen model's activations at $0.83$ where its own answer is below chance (a read-out failure), while fault type is no more decodable than from an untrained network (a representational one). Physical understanding and physically grounded decision-making are dissociable, and current models fail at both in different ways.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A model can track a physical system accurately and still be useless for acting on it. This gap is easy to state and hard to measure, because the two halves are usually evaluated separately: perception benchmarks score state estimation without asking what follows from it, and control benchmarks score task success without isolating whether failures come from misreading the physics or from mis-planning over a correct reading. For deployment the distinction is the whole question. An operator handed a diagnosis needs to know whether to trust the reading or the recommendation, and a researcher deciding where to spend the next unit of effort needs to know whether the bottleneck is representation or read-out.

We build a benchmark that keeps both halves on the same items so the gap between them is directly observable. Industrial robots are a good place to do this: they are physical systems with contact, payload dynamics, and controller constraints; they emit dense telemetry that fully determines their state; and, unlike most physical-reasoning testbeds, they come with an unambiguous notion of a correct action, since manufacturers publish the recovery procedure for each fault condition. That last property is what makes the decision half gradable rather than a matter of taste. FactoryBench is grounded in \textbf{FactoryWave}, a dense multivariate dataset from collaborative and industrial one-arm platforms (UR3 at 125\,Hz, KUKA KR10 at 83\,Hz) with synchronized setpoint, context, feedback, and effort signals; to our knowledge it is the first extensive anomaly dataset to (partly) cover an industrial robot, a class of machine normally locked behind proprietary controllers and plant confidentiality.

Counterfactual reasoning gets particular attention because it is where physical understanding is usually asserted rather than measured. Simulated environments make counterfactuals free but change the dynamics being tested; real hardware makes them expensive and, strictly, impossible, since two runs never share an identical pre-injection state. We approximate the do-operation empirically instead: for each baseline episode we re-execute the task 3--5 times with every controllable condition held fixed, inject the target fault at a fixed timestep, and retain the run whose pre-injection segment minimizes signature-kernel MMD against the baseline. This yields counterfactual ground truth recorded on the physical system rather than assumed from a simulator, at the cost of being a proxy whose quality we quantify rather than hide. Existing evaluations offer no equivalent: general benchmarks \cite{hendrycks2021mmlu,srivastava2023bigbench,yue2024mmmu} do not probe physical behavior, and time-series Q\&A benchmarks are typically synthetic or univariate and omit counterfactuals entirely, while neither specialized time-series models \cite{zhou2021informer,su2019omni,audibert2020usad} nor LLMs applied to dense numerical series \cite{gruver2023llmtime,jin2024timellm} have been tested across perception and decision-making jointly.
